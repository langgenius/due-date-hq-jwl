Hi Jeanette,

A heads-up your members may need this week: the IRS relief deadline for the December WA
storms is **Tuesday, Aug. 5** — but the IRS page's URL and body still say "May 1, 2026."
The Aug. 5 date only appears in an update banner added 5/1, so anyone who bookmarked the
notice early has a stale date.

One nuance we verified against the notice that catches payroll-heavy firms: the Aug. 5
postponement covers the Jan. 31 / Apr. 30 **quarterly payroll returns**, but deposit
abatement ended **Dec. 29, 2025** — deposits since then have been on the normal schedule.

We maintain a county-level tracker for this notice (with the IRS source quoted) at
duedatehq.com/irs-disaster-relief — free to link or excerpt for The Washington CPA or the
blog, no strings. Happy to supply a member-ready summary if useful.

## 5) Going Concern — tips/news desk

**Subject:** Story tip: the IRS "update banner" problem, live example ending Aug. 5

Hi — quick tip that fits your beat: when the IRS extends a disaster deadline, it edits the
old newsroom page with a banner but keeps the URL and body text unchanged. Live example: the
WA storms page URL literally ends "postponed-to-may-1-2026" while the operative deadline is
Aug. 5. Firms that bookmarked early are working from a date that's three months stale — and
the payroll wrinkle (941 returns postponed, deposits NOT — abatement ended Dec. 29) is the
kind of trap that turns into penalty notices.

We track these notices county-by-county at duedatehq.com/irs-disaster-relief and can share
the verbatim-quote receipts for all of it. Use freely with or without us; figured your
readers would enjoy the absurdity of the URL alone.
